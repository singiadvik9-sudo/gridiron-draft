# 4th and Pixels

A pixel-football game with real NFL starters (Madden-style ratings). Static page + a few serverless functions, built for Vercel.

## Modes
- **Quick game**: snake-draft 9 slots (QB, HB, 2 WR, TE, whole O-line, whole defense, kicker, and the rest of special teams: punter, long snapper and four coverage men); one of your drafted back, receivers or your defense's two starting corners returns kicks and punts, the best returner unless you pick another under your lineup (online rooms and tournaments use the best) from one shared pool, so every player lands on exactly one team, then play a friend with a 5-letter room code (each on their own device) or the CPU. The CPU drafts by value over replacement weighted by how much each slot moves team rating, and budgets under a cap. **Pick for me** and **Auto-draft** plan your whole lineup instead: on every pick they work out the best lineup still possible (the most team rating, each slot weighted by how much it moves the rating, for at most the cap room left; a knapsack over salaries in $0.1M steps), then take the planned player who would be hardest to replace if a rival took him (`planLineup`, `bestPick` in `lib/draft.js`; covered by `test_autodraft.js`). Without a cap that is simply the best player left at each open slot. Optional salary cap (Tight $330M / Standard $360M / Loose $400M; a real team's lineup costs about $274M): each player is paid his real contract (its average per year, from OverTheCap's data), and an O-line or defense package costs the sum of its 5 or 11 players (`lib/draft.js`).
- **Campaign**: GM of a real NFL team on the real 2026 schedule. You make roster moves and play your own games; the CPU sims everyone else. Every team is its real 53-man roster: its 2026 roster plus its injured-reserve list, with injuries ignored, cut down to the best 53 (enough players at every position first, then the highest rated; the extras become free agents), so a player on IR such as Myles Garrett is on his team (the Rams). The starters, O-line and defense, kicker, punter, coverage unit, returners and backups all come from those 53 (`build_rosters.py`: `fetch DIR` downloads the nflverse roster file and the EA ratings, `build DIR` rewrites the teams in `lib/data.json`). Ratings are Madden NFL 27 (EA's ratings database, Week 6; `build_rosters.py ratings DIR` re-rates everyone already in the data from the latest ratings without touching rosters or contracts); a player Madden 27 does not list yet keeps his last Madden NFL 26 rating, and anyone in neither is estimated from draft slot and experience and marked as an estimate. Fullbacks only fill a running back minimum, since the game has no fullback spot. **Ends and tackles** are positions of their own (DE, DT): Madden's edge rushers are ends and its interior linemen tackles, whatever the scheme. A 4-3 starts two ends, two tackles and three linebackers; a 3-4 starts three tackles (LDT, NT, RDT), two ends standing up at the edges (LEDGE, REDGE, where it used to play outside linebackers) and two inside linebackers, so no team plays an end at linebacker any more. Each slot also has a job on the field (`slotRole`, `FRONT` in `lib/league.js`): the 3-4's stand-up ends play the edge spots as before. Saves from before the split are upgraded when they load (`upgradePositions`; covered by `test_defense.js`). **Salaries are real**: every player is paid his real contract's average per year through its last season (OverTheCap's contract data via nflverse; `build_rosters.py salaries`), 1,800 of the 1,850 players in the data. Anyone without one, everyone the game makes up (rookies, depth fill-ins) and anyone whose contract runs out (he re-signs in the offseason for one to four seasons) is paid the real market for his position and rating (`SAL` in `lib/data.json`: the median real salary within 3 rating points, never falling as the rating rises; `salaryOfPos` in `lib/draft.js`). The free-agent pool starts with the players who did not make a 53 and the real players who were cut. **Payroll**: every player is paid his salary (rating and position), free agents included, and signing one needs room under the cap: $375M in the campaign and NFL-team leagues (with real contracts a 53-man roster with its IR players costs $180M-$398M, so the four priciest teams start over it and must clear room first), the league's own draft cap in draft leagues with one. Releasing a player frees his salary at once. The Free agents tab shows each player's salary and your room; the Roster tab shows your payroll (`payroll`, `capRoom`, `signFA` in `lib/league.js`; covered by `test_payroll.js`). Rosters start full, so to take on more players than you send in a trade (or sign a free agent) you release someone first; a CPU team that ends up over 53 after a trade cuts its least valuable player. Kickers and punters are normal roster spots you can trade, sign or release. There is no returner position: each team names one of its running backs, receivers or corners to return kicks and punts (Roster tab; the best returner by default, rated from Madden speed, agility and change of direction, `returnerOf` in `lib/league.js`; covered by `test_returners.js`). **Usage**: instead of a fixed two receivers, one back and one tight end, every back, receiver and tight end has a usage, his share of his group's snaps, which the coach sets on the Roster tab (by default the best play most: receivers 95/80/20/5, backs 65/30/5, tight ends 85/15). On every run or pass snap the engine draws who fills the HB, the two WR spots and the TE spot from the game seed (so the screen, the live sim and the league server agree): each player's chance is his usage, nudged by how well he fits the play (speed for deep shots, overall for quick and intermediate routes, speed for outside runs), and on passing downs a receiver can take the TE spot in the slot (`personnel` in `lib/engine.js`). Stats and box scores credit whoever was on the field, and every snap is counted. Quick-game squads keep their five. **Fatigue**: backs, receivers and tight ends in a rotation tire as they stay on the field (backs fastest, better players a little slower) and recover off it, at a kick, between quarters and mostly at halftime. A tired player is drawn a little less often (his weight falls from 1 to 0.35 as his energy runs out: a starting back at 30% energy still plays about half his usual snaps) and plays up to 14 points below his rating when spent, so fresher players rotate in as a drive goes on. Energy is part of the game state (the replay, the live sim and the league server agree) and shows in the in-game Rosters view; quick-game squads do not tire (`fatigueAfter`, `energy` in `lib/engine.js`; covered by `test_fatigue.js`). **Starters**: on the Roster tab the coach can name who starts at any position (Start / Starter ✓), for example a rookie to develop him; named starters go ahead of better-rated players in the lineup, and for backs, receivers and tight ends the default usage follows that order. A traded or released player is nobody's named starter (`setStarter` in `lib/league.js`; covered by `test_starters.js`). **Injuries**: after every game each player who played can get hurt, a chance per snap that comes to about 1.5 injuries per team per game, drawn from the game seed (every screen and the league server agree); about 70% last a week or two, some up to twelve, a few the season. The injured sit (lineup, rotation, returner) and heal a week at a time; the news says who is hurt. **Injured reserve** takes an injured player off the 53 (he stays on the team and its payroll) so you can sign someone; he stays at least 4 weeks and comes back when healthy (Roster tab: Place on IR / Activate). CPU teams put long injuries on IR, bring players back and sign a free agent for an empty starting spot. Injured players are worth less in trades. Everyone is healthy again in the offseason, and custom leagues start with everyone healthy (`injuries`, `injuryWeek`, `placeIR`, `activateIR` in `lib/league.js`; covered by `test_injuries.js`). Season ends with playoffs, then ageing/development (scaled by playing time: a young player's growth runs from about half for one who sat to 1.3x for a full-time starter, and a veteran who plays declines a little slower; `snapShare`), a rookie draft (random names; each class has a few future superstars with potential 93-99, a couple of dozen future stars, starters and depth, rookies rated 50-81. Potential is scouted, not known: the board, your roster, CPU teams and trade values all see the scouts' estimate, marked `pot ~`, a few points off the truth. About one in eight who look like stars are busts, and about nine sleepers a class look like late-rounders but can become stars. The true potential shows after a player's first season, with Sleeper and Bust tags and news; `genProspects`, `potSeen` in `lib/league.js`, covered by `test_draftclass.js`), preseason roster cuts, and the next season. Trade with CPU teams (they judge value, age and need), sign free agents, release players. **Find offers** on the Trades tab can shop for one position: pick it and each CPU team offers its best player there who would start for you (or come close), for the cheapest package of your players and picks it would accept, never more than 1.5x his value and never a player at that same position (`cpuOffers(L, team, pos)` in `lib/league.js`; covered by `test_offers.js`). Leagues get the same through `op: 'cpuoffers'` with `pos`. **Trading block**: press Shop next to any of your players on the Roster tab (up to 8) and the Trades tab lists what each CPU team would bid for him: its most valuable player or pick (or two) that it still accepts, never more than 1.3x his value, best bids first, with an Accept button (`setBlock`, `blockBids` in `lib/league.js`; leagues use `op: 'block'` and `op: 'blockbids'`; covered by `test_block.js`). Players who are traded or released leave the block. Saves: 5 slots on your account, or 3 on this device for guests.
- **Leagues** (accounts only): the manager creates a league and shares the code. In the lobby the manager toggles divisions on/off (and how many), CPU teams on/off, NFL-team entry vs. a snake draft, trades on/off (plus deadline), games per team, playoff size, draft order and who sits in which division. Humans can play each other live or the manager can sim the week. Each finished season is written to every manager's account (record, rank, champion flag, personal stat lines) until they delete it on the Account page. If a manager does not show up, the league manager can make either team forfeit any open game that week (Manager tools → Open games this week): the other team wins 1-0 and the week moves on. Covered by `test_forfeit.js`. **Draft leagues** (made from now on): each team takes one NFL team's whole O-line and one whole defense as a single pick each (starters and backups; the defense brings its 4-3 or 3-4 scheme; it costs the sum of its players' salaries), and its quarterback, backs, receivers, tight ends, kicker and punter one player at a time, over 12, 16 or 20 rounds. Salary caps for these drafts are $200M / $230M / $260M (with real salaries an elite defense alone costs $150M-$175M). Linemen and defenders nobody had stay free agents. CPU teams and managers on auto-draft plan the best starting lineup the cap still allows (the same planner as the quick game's Pick for me, `autoPickFor` in `lib/league.js`), then fill depth; each pick keeps enough room for the picks still to come at the price of the cheapest players left, and a team that cannot afford anyone passes. Leagues made before this keep drafting every player one by one (they have no `units` flag). Covered by `test_unitdraft.js`.
- **Tournaments** (accounts only): single elimination, best-of per round (early / semis / final), snake-drafted teams (optional salary cap, up to 16 teams, CPU entrants auto-pick, manager can auto-pick for anyone who stalls) or real NFL teams, CPU entrants, custom seeding (drag-free: up/down arrows, join order, random, by rating), manager overrides. Byes go to top seeds when the field isn't a power of two.

## Playing it yourself (live control)
**Game clock.** Four 2:00 quarters (overtime is one 2:00 period, first score wins, 2 timeouts), played with NFL stoppage rules: the clock stops on incomplete passes, out-of-bounds plays, scores, and changes of possession, and runs otherwise. There is no delay of game. Instead, the clock that runs between the end of a play and the next snap is capped at **5 seconds** (the play itself takes its own few seconds on top, so a typical running play costs about 8-9 s of clock). Each team has 3 timeouts per half; tick the **Timeout** switch (key `T`) before the snap to use one, which also gives back the clock that was running down. Offenses also get **Spike** (stops the clock, costs a down) and **Kneel** (runs the clock, loses a yard or so). A quarter always ends on a snap, never between them. The CPU kneels, spikes, hurries up and calls timeouts at sensible moments (`E.cpuTO`). Spike and kneel, like kicks, are automatic and not live-playable. Covered by `test_clock.js`.

**Play calls.** The call screen is split into sections, and each section holds real named plays. Offense: Inside Run (Inside Zone, Dive, Power, Draw), Outside Run (Outside Zone, Toss Sweep, Counter), Quick Pass (Slants, Hitches, Mesh, Stick), Medium Pass (Dig, Curl-Flat, Flood, Y-Cross), Deep Shot (Four Verticals, Post-Corner, Streak, Play-Action Bomb), Screen (HB, Bubble, Tunnel), Trick Plays (Flea Flicker, Hook and Lateral, Halfback Pass, WR Reverse, and on 4th down a Fake Punt) and Clock & Kicks (Spike, Kneel, Punt, Field Goal). Defense: Stack the Box (4-4 Stack, Goal Line, Run Blitz), Zone (Cover 2, 3, 4), Man (Cover 1, 2-Man, Press Man), Blitz (All-Out, Zone Blitz, Corner Blitz), Prevent (Prevent, Soft Zone). Arrow keys switch section, number keys pick the play. In `lib/engine.js` each play tweaks the numbers of its section (`ym`, `base`, `tw`, `vs` ...) and `E.matchup(defCall, offPlay)` says who wins (see the Matchups table in the game); in `lib/live.js` each play has its own routes (`ROUTES`) and each defensive call its own coverage layout, so live snaps look and play differently. The CPU picks plays by down, distance and clock. Trick plays hit big against the wrong defense, but the defense remembers: every trick a team has run this game makes the next one easier to read (`state.tricks`). On a snap played by hand they play out for real (`trickOff` in `lib/live.js`): the **Flea Flicker** hands off, the back runs at the line and pitches it back while the defense comes up for the run, then the quarterback throws deep; the **Halfback Pass** tosses it to the back, who rolls out and throws (you control him and throw with `A` `S` `D` `F`); on the **Hook and Lateral** the hook man pitches it to the receiver trailing him (`E` when you have him, or on his own when a tackler closes in; a defender right there can knock it loose); the **WR Reverse** sends a receiver in motion behind the quarterback the other way while the back fakes, and the front and the linebackers flow with the fake until they read it. Covered by `test_plays.js` and `test_fun.js`.

**Calling plays on the field.** The call screen sits on the field itself, Madden style: it covers the field while you pick a play, steps aside while a play is shown, and shrinks to a strip at the bottom while you wait for the other side (on phones it sits under the field). The side column keeps the last play, the log and, during a snap you play by hand, your controls.

**Coach's picks.** Every call screen opens on the coach's pick (like Ask Madden): three suggestions from different sections (on offense always a run and a pass), the best one starred, with the reason, e.g. "Expect Zone on 3rd and long (they have shown it about 40% of the time). Y-Cross gains the most against what they are likely to call." `G` calls the starred one. `E.suggest` reads what the other team has called in this kind of spot this game (from the log, never this snap's call), blends it with what teams usually do, plays every option out against that with the stat engine (seeded, so the same spot gets the same advice, and fast: about 10 ms) and scores it in expected points, a little risk-averse. On 4th down, at the end of a half and on kickoffs it gives the obvious call (kick the field goal, punt, spike, kneel, onside). The coach does not call trick plays. Covered by `test_fun.js`.

**Turnovers.** An interception, a lost fumble or a turnover on downs stops the action for a moment: a band across the field in the colour of the team that won the ball says TURNOVER, whose ball it is now and who made the play ("PICKED OFF BY JAYLEN WATSON", "FORCED BY DRAKE THOMAS"), and the log marks the play. On a snap played by hand the pick gets a "PICKED OFF!" call-out and a beat of slow motion the moment it happens, before the return plays out; a fumble the offense falls on says so.

**Pace.** Plays animate quicker and the pause after each one is short. Kickoffs kick deep on their own unless you are trailing in the fourth quarter (then you choose deep or onside). **Hurry-up** (button, or key `R`) runs your last call again with no huddle.

**On fire.** A back, receiver or quarterback with 15+ yards (or a touchdown) on two touches in a row catches fire: he plays 8 points better for 6 snaps, shows flames on the field and is named over the play calls. Stopping him for no gain, or picking off a pass to him, cools him off. Fire is part of the game state, like fatigue, so the replay, the live sim and the league server agree (`onFire`, `fireAfter` in `lib/engine.js`; covered by `test_fun.js`).

**Catches and confidence.** On the field every catch is told apart: over two or more defenders (contested), at full stretch (a diving grab: he lays out), with both feet down on the sideline (toe tap), or over the shoulder on a lob. The receiver leaps (or dives), a ring bursts from the catch, the grab is named over him and in the play's line. The stat engine has contested catches too, about one completion in ten (drawn from a seeded stream of its own, so no other result changes). A contested catch gives the receiver **confidence**: +5 (+7 over three defenders) for his next 5 snaps, shown with sparks around him on the field and a line over the play calls; it stacks with being on fire. Like fire it is part of the game state (`st.conf`, `confAfter` in `lib/engine.js`). Covered by `test_fun.js`.

**Two-point conversions.** On the call screen, *After a TD* (key `V`, remembered) switches between kicking the extra point and going for two; it rides along with every call (`two` in the move), so it counts for whichever snap scores, a pick-six included. A try from the 2 is about a coin flip, a little better for the stronger offense against the weaker defense, drawn from a seeded stream of its own so it moves no other result. CPU teams follow the two-point chart in the second half (`cpuGo2`). Covered by `test_fun.js`.

**Team colours.** Every team plays in its own colours: real franchises (campaign, leagues, tournaments, or a quick-game team named after one) in theirs, any other team in a colour picked from its name. When the two would look alike the second team wears its other colour, or white (`applyTeamColors` in `src/game.js`).

**The play has to end.** If the quarterback still has the ball after about 2.3 seconds, zone defenders with nobody left to cover come after him; after 4 seconds everyone in zone but the free safety does (`PRO.lateT`, `PRO.lateT2` in `lib/live.js`). Holding the ball now gets you sacked in about 4.4 seconds instead of standing there for 11.

**Celebrations.** Touchdowns, interceptions, fumbles and players catching fire shake the field (touchdowns and fire throw confetti); sacks and big hits get a smaller shake.

On every call screen there is a **Control your players** switch (key `C`, remembered between plays). Off: you only call plays and the stat engine decides the snap, as before. On: both sides' players run on the field in real time (`lib/live.js`, 22 players at 20 ticks a second). The offense plays the quarterback (arrow keys to move; every other control is on the left side of the keyboard: `A` `S` `D` `F` (left hand on the home row; `1-4` also work) throw a bullet to the receiver tagged with that letter, `Shift` + the letter lobs it: a lob floats over underneath defenders and leads the receiver, but hangs in the air for the safeties; `Space` throws it away), then whoever has the ball (`Space` jukes, `Q` spins: harder to tackle for a moment at some speed). Run the quarterback past the line of scrimmage to scramble: he becomes a runner (no more passes, tackled there it is a run, not a sack), and receivers whose routes are done work back toward him. The defense plays one defender (`Space` dives, `Q` lands a big hit, which tackles and forces fumbles more often but leaves him stumbling if it misses, `E` switches to the one nearest the ball, which at the snap is a lineman; steering roughly at the ball carrier guides you onto his path). **Pass rush:** when a blocker has you, a meter under your player shows who is winning; `Q` bull rushes (builds leverage) and `Space` swims past him, which works more often with the meter in the green or the moment he gets his hands on you, and leaves you stonewalled if it fails. Beat him and you burst toward the quarterback. **No free sacks:** a defender who was not rushing at the snap and comes after the quarterback (yours, or a delayed blitz) is picked up by a spare lineman or the back, and a quarterback who sees a blitzer coming free throws hot. A rusher you play well gets home on about 6% of dropbacks, a corner blitz about 5% (it was 30-50% for anyone who ran straight at the quarterback). While a pass is in the air a ring marks where it will come down. Everyone you don't control moves on his own, following the play call. Touch screens get an on-screen pad. "Let the computer play it" hands your player back mid-snap. Field goals, kneels and spikes stay automatic, but **kickoffs and punts can be run back by hand**: when the receiving player has *Control your players* on, the ball is caught and he runs it back against the kicking team's coverage with his ten blockers (arrow keys run, `Space` juke, and on a punt `E` calls a fair catch). It works on every punt and on every kickoff that is not a touchback, including the ones after scores and at halftime; if you didn't switch control on, or the computer is receiving, the old dice decide. Which side runs a return is read from that side's latest recorded call (`E.lastCtl`), so every screen and the server agree without extra messages; the inputs travel as move number `1000 + n` and are verified like a live snap (`n-rv` in the replay). Either player can have it on or off on any play, vs. a friend or the CPU, in quick games, leagues and tournaments.

How it stays in sync (and honest): the snap is a pure function of `seed + play number + squads + calls + each controller's input trace`. Each player streams inputs through the room (`op: 'live'` in `api/room.js`); an input takes effect ~0.5 s later on every screen (input delay, which raises itself if the connection is slow) and a screen only advances once it has both players' inputs (lockstep). The traces are stored with the game, so the server replays the exact same snap when it verifies a league or tournament result. If a player vanishes mid-snap, the other can hand their player to the computer after 5 s. Reloading mid-snap rebuilds it from the room.

**One-screen layout and full screen.** During a game the page fits the window with no scrolling: on a wide screen the field is on the left and the play selector, last play and log are on the right; on narrower or taller windows the field sits above a compact call panel (hover a play for its full description). The **Full screen** button on the field (or key `F`) uses the browser's full screen where it exists and drops the header; on iPhone, which has no page full screen, it still switches to the header-free layout. The box score screen after the final whistle scrolls normally.

**Kickoffs and onside kicks.** After every score, at the half and at the start of overtime the game has a kickoff step of its own (`state.kick`, `E.KICKS`): the kicking team picks *Kick it deep* or *Onside kick* (keys `1` / `2`), the receiving team waits. A deep kick is a touchback or a return as before (run back by hand when the receiver has control on). An onside kick works about one time in eight (kicker's coverage unit against the receiving unit, 5-25%): recovered, the kicking team keeps the ball at its own 45; otherwise the receivers take it at their own 45. The computer onside kicks only when it trails by 1-16 late in the fourth quarter (`E.cpuKick`).

**Fumbles and pass breakups.** Fumbles are a real event now: roughly one tackle in 35 shakes the ball loose (about 1.5 fumbles a game, half of them lost, close to the NFL) (more on sacks, where the quarterback is holding it out, and on dive tackles), and half the time the offense falls on it, so you see "strip sack", "knocked loose" and "falls on it" lines in both the dice and the live sim (`PRO.fum`, `PRO.fumSack`; entries carry `ev: 'fumble'` or `fum: 'kept'`). A defender whose hands are in the way of a thrown ball gets a chance at it **on the way to the receiver**, not only where it lands (`contestFlight` in `lib/live.js`; `PRO.fly*`): he can pick it off or knock it down, and a defender you control with `Space` (jump) reaches higher and further. The ball leaves the quarterback's hand at head height and travels over the line, so linemen (hands up to about 8 feet) only bat down a low throw, which a quarterback makes now and then, more often under pressure (about 1% of throws are batted at the line). About a quarter of passes are now broken up and the dice sim credits breakups to a defender too. League stats track `pbu`.

**Box score.** Every finished game (quick game, campaign, league, tournament) shows team totals and the leaders on each side under the final score: passing, rushing, receiving, defence (sacks, interceptions, breakups, forced fumbles) and the kicker (`E.boxScore`).

**Pass blocking.** The offensive line is tuned so a pass play gives the quarterback time (`PRO` in `lib/live.js`): a four-man rush takes about six seconds to get to a standing quarterback, a blitz about two and a half; spare linemen help a teammate who is losing, blitzers arrive a few ticks late, and defenders get a small edge at the catch point so completions do not run away.

**Controls on screen.** The player you run has a pulsing ring, a bobbing arrow and a name tag, receivers carry big numbers while the quarterback has the ball, and a key strip along the bottom of the field lists exactly what your keys do at that moment.

## Schedules after the first year
2026 uses the real NFL slate. Every later campaign season is generated (`nflMatchups` in `lib/league.js`): 6 division games, 4 against one rotating same-conference division, 4 against one rotating other-conference division, 2 same-conference games against the two divisions not in the rotation, and a 17th game against another other-conference division. The 2 same-conference games and the 17th game match teams by **last season's division finish** (first place plays first place, and so on), taken from the regular-season records (`divisionRanks`). Weeks come from `assignWeeks`: the games are packed into 17 weeks in the order they are listed, which always fits, and then the weeks are shuffled so the six division games are spread through the season, never two weeks in a row (all teams play their division games in the same weeks). Custom leagues use a plain round-robin instead. Covered by `test_sched.js`.

## Your players' stats
The Stats tab (campaign and leagues) opens with a **Your players** panel: passing, rushing, receiving and defensive lines for your team for the season so far, any single game this season, any past season, or your career with the team. A team picker on the same panel shows any other team's players too; for teams nobody runs only this season's totals exist, so single games, past seasons and career are offered only for teams a person runs. Totals for the whole league are kept as before, but game-by-game lines are only stored for teams a person runs (so saves stay small), and each season's line is archived on the player when the season ends. Games played before this feature existed have totals but no game lines.

## Draft picks in trades
Every team always owns one pick per round for the next **three** drafts it can still trade (7 rounds in the campaign, the league's rookie-round setting in leagues): during the season that is this year's draft and the next two; once this year's draft starts, its picks lock and a fourth year appears right away, so three years stay tradeable through the draft, preseason and the next season. Picks appear above the player lists on the Trades tab, one row per year, one chip per round; the number on each chip is its trade value and hovering shows the expected overall slot. A pick can be part of any trade, with CPU teams or between managers, alone or mixed with players (up to 8 assets each way). Each pick's value (`pickValue` in `lib/league.js`, same units as `playerValue`) comes from three things: the **round** (value follows the rookie a pick in that slot is expected to produce), the **projected finish** of the team it originally belongs to (for this year's draft: lineup rating, blended with the record as the season goes on, and the real draft order once the season is over; for later drafts: the team's **overall rating** alone, so the lowest-rated team's future picks project first in each round and the highest-rated team's last), and nothing else: picks for later drafts are not discounted for being further away, so a team's pick keeps its value from year to year. When the rookie draft runs, each slot goes to whoever owns that pick and spent picks disappear. Once the draft has started, that year's picks cannot be traded. Covered by `test_picks.js`, `ui_picks.py` and the pick checks in `test_api.js`.

Note: a live snap costs roughly 10 Redis commands per second per controlling player while it runs (about 100-200 commands for a snap), so a game played entirely live uses a few thousand.

## Deploy
1. Push this folder to GitHub, import it in Vercel. Framework: **Other**, no build command, output directory `public` (already in `vercel.json`; `public/index.html` is committed pre-built).
2. Add **Upstash Redis** from the Vercel Marketplace (it sets `KV_REST_API_URL` / `KV_REST_API_TOKEN`, or `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL` / `_TOKEN`). Without it the functions use per-instance memory, which only works for local testing.
3. Redeploy.

**Cloudflare instead of Upstash:** create a D1 database (Cloudflare dashboard → Storage & Databases → D1), then add three Vercel environment variables: `CF_ACCOUNT_ID`, `CF_D1_DATABASE_ID`, and `CF_API_TOKEN` (an API token with *D1: Edit*). The table is created automatically. If these are set, they're used instead of Upstash. Note Cloudflare's REST API allows about 1,200 requests per 5 minutes per token, which caps how many people can have a league or tournament page open at once (each polls every 5 s); Upstash has no such cap.

Accounts are name + password (scrypt) with a 30-day session token. Nothing else is stored about people.

## How results stay honest
Games are deterministic: every client and the server replay `seed + squads + picks`. CPU opponents are deterministic too, so a human-vs-CPU result is verified server-side from the human's picks alone; human-vs-human results are replayed from the picks stored in the room. A reported result that doesn't replay to a finished game is rejected. Quick-game and tournament drafts are validated on the server (turn order, one owner per player, slot limits, cap).

## Costs / limits
Leagues and tournaments poll a tiny version key every 5 s while open. Free-tier Upstash (10k commands/day) is enough for casual groups; raise `ms` in `poll()` (`src/hub/01_core.js`) if you hit it. League documents are stored gzipped (~100-300 KB).

**Camera on live snaps.** While the quarterback holds the ball the camera leans downfield and zooms out (down to about 52 yards across) so his deepest target stays in the picture; it frames the throw while the ball is in the air. A receiver still out of the picture gets a marker on the edge with his throw key and how far away he is. The player you steer starts and cuts quicker than the others. (The 3D camera views were removed.) Covered by `test_controls.js`.

## Change log
Every update gets an entry at the top of `CHANGELOG` in `src/hub/00_changelog.js` (newest first; the id is the date, with -2, -3 for more releases the same day; written for players). The first time someone visits after an update they see **What's new** with every release since the one they last saw: an account remembers it on the server (`op: 'seen'` in `api/auth.js`, so it shows once on any device), a guest in the browser. A brand-new visitor is not shown the history; someone who played before the change log existed sees the latest release. The whole log is under What's new at the top. The releases so far were written from the git history.
