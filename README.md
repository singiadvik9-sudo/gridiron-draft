# 4th and Pixels

A pixel-football game with real NFL starters (Madden-style ratings). Static page + a few serverless functions, built for Vercel.

## Modes
- **Quick game**: snake-draft 11 slots (QB, HB, 2 WR, TE, whole O-line, whole defense, kicker, punt returner, kick returner, and the rest of special teams: punter, long snapper and four coverage men) from one shared pool, so every player lands on exactly one team, then play a friend with a 5-letter room code (each on their own device) or the CPU. The CPU drafts by value over replacement weighted by how much each slot moves team rating, and budgets under a cap. Optional salary cap (Tight $320M / Standard $350M / Loose $390M; a real team's lineup costs about $265M): each player's salary comes from rating and position (QB highest, then WR/DE, CB/DT/OL, LB, TE/S, HB), and an O-line or defense package costs the sum of its 5 or 11 players (`lib/draft.js`).
- **Campaign**: GM of a real NFL team on the real 2026 schedule. You make roster moves and play your own games; the CPU sims everyone else. Every team is its real 53-man roster: its 2026 roster plus its injured-reserve list, with injuries ignored, cut down to the best 53 (enough players at every position first, then the highest rated; the extras become free agents), so a player on IR such as Myles Garrett is on his team (the Rams). The starters, O-line and defense, kicker, punter, coverage unit, returners and backups all come from those 53 (`build_rosters.py`: `fetch DIR` downloads the nflverse roster file and the EA ratings, `build DIR` rewrites the teams in `lib/data.json`). Ratings are Madden NFL 27 (EA's ratings database, Week 4); a player Madden 27 does not list yet keeps his last Madden NFL 26 rating, and anyone in neither is estimated from draft slot and experience and marked as an estimate. Fullbacks only fill a running back minimum, since the game has no fullback spot. Salaries follow from rating and position (`salaryOfPos` in `lib/draft.js`; in a 3-4 the outside linebackers are paid like edge rushers). The free-agent pool starts with the players who did not make a 53 and the real players who were cut. Rosters start full, so to take on more players than you send in a trade (or sign a free agent) you release someone first; a CPU team that ends up over 53 after a trade cuts its least valuable player. Kickers, punters, kick returners and punt returners are normal roster spots you can trade, sign or release (a team with no returner of its own uses its franchise's real one). Season ends with playoffs, then ageing/development, an auto-generated rookie draft (random names and ratings), preseason roster cuts, and the next season. Trade with CPU teams (they judge value, age and need), sign free agents, release players. **Find offers** on the Trades tab can shop for one position: pick it and each CPU team offers its best player there who would start for you (or come close), for the cheapest package of your players and picks it would accept, never more than 1.5x his value and never a player at that same position (`cpuOffers(L, team, pos)` in `lib/league.js`; covered by `test_offers.js`). Leagues get the same through `op: 'cpuoffers'` with `pos`. **Trading block**: press Shop next to any of your players on the Roster tab (up to 8) and the Trades tab lists what each CPU team would bid for him: its most valuable player or pick (or two) that it still accepts, never more than 1.3x his value, best bids first, with an Accept button (`setBlock`, `blockBids` in `lib/league.js`; leagues use `op: 'block'` and `op: 'blockbids'`; covered by `test_block.js`). Players who are traded or released leave the block. Saves: 5 slots on your account, or 3 on this device for guests.
- **Leagues** (accounts only): the manager creates a league and shares the code. In the lobby the manager toggles divisions on/off (and how many), CPU teams on/off, NFL-team entry vs. a snake draft of every player, trades on/off (plus deadline), games per team, playoff size, draft order and who sits in which division. Humans can play each other live or the manager can sim the week. Each finished season is written to every manager's account (record, rank, champion flag, personal stat lines) until they delete it on the Account page. If a manager does not show up, the league manager can make either team forfeit any open game that week (Manager tools → Open games this week): the other team wins 1-0 and the week moves on. Covered by `test_forfeit.js`.
- **Tournaments** (accounts only): single elimination, best-of per round (early / semis / final), snake-drafted teams (optional salary cap, up to 16 teams, CPU entrants auto-pick, manager can auto-pick for anyone who stalls) or real NFL teams, CPU entrants, custom seeding (drag-free: up/down arrows, join order, random, by rating), manager overrides. Byes go to top seeds when the field isn't a power of two.

## Playing it yourself (live control)
**Game clock.** Four 2:00 quarters (overtime is one 2:00 period, first score wins, 2 timeouts), played with NFL stoppage rules: the clock stops on incomplete passes, out-of-bounds plays, scores, and changes of possession, and runs otherwise. There is no delay of game. Instead, the clock that runs between the end of a play and the next snap is capped at **5 seconds** (the play itself takes its own few seconds on top, so a typical running play costs about 8-9 s of clock). Each team has 3 timeouts per half; tick the **Timeout** switch (key `T`) before the snap to use one, which also gives back the clock that was running down. Offenses also get **Spike** (stops the clock, costs a down) and **Kneel** (runs the clock, loses a yard or so). A quarter always ends on a snap, never between them. The CPU kneels, spikes, hurries up and calls timeouts at sensible moments (`E.cpuTO`). Spike and kneel, like kicks, are automatic and not live-playable. Covered by `test_clock.js`.

**Play calls.** The call screen is split into sections, and each section holds real named plays. Offense: Inside Run (Inside Zone, Dive, Power, Draw), Outside Run (Outside Zone, Toss Sweep, Counter), Quick Pass (Slants, Hitches, Mesh, Stick), Medium Pass (Dig, Curl-Flat, Flood, Y-Cross), Deep Shot (Four Verticals, Post-Corner, Streak, Play-Action Bomb), Screen (HB, Bubble, Tunnel) and Clock & Kicks (Spike, Kneel, Punt, Field Goal). Defense: Stack the Box (4-4 Stack, Goal Line, Run Blitz), Zone (Cover 2, 3, 4), Man (Cover 1, 2-Man, Press Man), Blitz (All-Out, Zone Blitz, Corner Blitz), Prevent (Prevent, Soft Zone). Arrow keys switch section, number keys pick the play. In `lib/engine.js` each play tweaks the numbers of its section (`ym`, `base`, `tw`, `vs` ...) and `E.matchup(defCall, offPlay)` says who wins (see the Matchups table in the game); in `lib/live.js` each play has its own routes (`ROUTES`) and each defensive call its own coverage layout, so live snaps look and play differently. The CPU picks plays by down, distance and clock. Covered by `test_plays.js`.

On every call screen there is a **Control your players** switch (key `C`, remembered between plays). Off: you only call plays and the stat engine decides the snap, as before. On: both sides' players run on the field in real time (`lib/live.js`, 22 players at 20 ticks a second). The offense plays the quarterback (WASD or arrows to move, `J` `K` `L` `;` (right hand on the home row; `1-4` also work) throw to the receiver tagged with that letter, `Space` throws it away), then whoever has the ball (`Space` jukes). The defense plays one defender (`Space` dives, `E` switches to the one nearest the ball). Everyone you don't control moves on his own, following the play call. Touch screens get an on-screen pad. "Let the computer play it" hands your player back mid-snap. Field goals, kneels and spikes stay automatic, but **kickoffs and punts can be run back by hand**: when the receiving player has *Control your players* on, the ball is caught and he runs it back against the kicking team's coverage with his ten blockers (`WASD` run, `Space` juke, and on a punt `E` calls a fair catch). It works on every punt and on every kickoff that is not a touchback, including the ones after scores and at halftime; if you didn't switch control on, or the computer is receiving, the old dice decide. Which side runs a return is read from that side's latest recorded call (`E.lastCtl`), so every screen and the server agree without extra messages; the inputs travel as move number `1000 + n` and are verified like a live snap (`n-rv` in the replay). Either player can have it on or off on any play, vs. a friend or the CPU, in quick games, leagues and tournaments.

How it stays in sync (and honest): the snap is a pure function of `seed + play number + squads + calls + each controller's input trace`. Each player streams inputs through the room (`op: 'live'` in `api/room.js`); an input takes effect ~0.5 s later on every screen (input delay, which raises itself if the connection is slow) and a screen only advances once it has both players' inputs (lockstep). The traces are stored with the game, so the server replays the exact same snap when it verifies a league or tournament result. If a player vanishes mid-snap, the other can hand their player to the computer after 5 s. Reloading mid-snap rebuilds it from the room.

**One-screen layout and full screen.** During a game the page fits the window with no scrolling: on a wide screen the field is on the left and the play selector, last play and log are on the right; on narrower or taller windows the field sits above a compact call panel (hover a play for its full description). The **Full screen** button on the field (or key `F`) uses the browser's full screen where it exists and drops the header; on iPhone, which has no page full screen, it still switches to the header-free layout. The box score screen after the final whistle scrolls normally.

**Kickoffs and onside kicks.** After every score, at the half and at the start of overtime the game has a kickoff step of its own (`state.kick`, `E.KICKS`): the kicking team picks *Kick it deep* or *Onside kick* (keys `1` / `2`), the receiving team waits. A deep kick is a touchback or a return as before (run back by hand when the receiver has control on). An onside kick works about one time in eight (kicker's coverage unit against the receiving unit, 5-25%): recovered, the kicking team keeps the ball at its own 45; otherwise the receivers take it at their own 45. The computer onside kicks only when it trails by 1-16 late in the fourth quarter (`E.cpuKick`).

**Fumbles and pass breakups.** Fumbles are a real event now: roughly one tackle in 25 shakes the ball loose (more on sacks, where the quarterback is holding it out, and on dive tackles), and half the time the offense falls on it, so you see "strip sack", "knocked loose" and "falls on it" lines in both the dice and the live sim (`PRO.fum`, `PRO.fumSack`; entries carry `ev: 'fumble'` or `fum: 'kept'`). A defender whose hands are in the way of a thrown ball gets a chance at it **on the way to the receiver**, not only where it lands (`contestFlight` in `lib/live.js`; `PRO.fly*`): he can pick it off or knock it down, and a defender you control with `Space` (jump) reaches higher and further. About a quarter of passes are now broken up and the dice sim credits breakups to a defender too. League stats track `pbu`.

**Box score.** Every finished game (quick game, campaign, league, tournament) shows team totals and the leaders on each side under the final score: passing, rushing, receiving, defence (sacks, interceptions, breakups, forced fumbles) and the kicker (`E.boxScore`).

**Pass blocking.** The offensive line is tuned so a pass play gives the quarterback time (`PRO` in `lib/live.js`): a four-man rush takes about six seconds to get to a standing quarterback, a blitz about two and a half; spare linemen help a teammate who is losing, blitzers arrive a few ticks late, and defenders get a small edge at the catch point so completions do not run away.

**Controls on screen.** The player you run has a pulsing ring, a bobbing arrow and a name tag, receivers carry big numbers while the quarterback has the ball, and a key strip along the bottom of the field lists exactly what your keys do at that moment.

**Camera angles / 3D views.** A View menu on the field (and the `V` key) switches between six angles: *Sideline* (the classic pixel view), *Broadcast 3D*, *Behind offense* (down the field), *Behind defense*, *Player cam* (over the shoulder of whoever you control) and *Overhead*. Everything except Sideline is a real 3D scene (low-poly players, stands, goalposts, ball). The choice is remembered. three.js is vendored in `public/vendor/` and only loaded the first time a 3D view is picked; without WebGL the game stays in 2D. Controls are camera-relative, so "up" always means away from the camera. In plays resolved by dice (no live sim) only the ball carrier, receiver and ball move in 3D. A pass in 3D leaves the passer's raised hand (above his helmet), follows the throw's arc and comes down to the catcher's hands, so it travels over the defenders in between (`passY` in `src/field3d.js`; the arc is the live sim's own, `peak` in `throwTo`, and dice passes use the same formula from their length). This is drawing only: the sim and its verified results are unchanged. `ui_views.py` tests every view and its stick mapping.

## Schedules after the first year
2026 uses the real NFL slate. Every later campaign season is generated (`nflMatchups` in `lib/league.js`): 6 division games, 4 against one rotating same-conference division, 4 against one rotating other-conference division, 2 same-conference games against the two divisions not in the rotation, and a 17th game against another other-conference division. The 2 same-conference games and the 17th game match teams by **last season's division finish** (first place plays first place, and so on), taken from the regular-season records (`divisionRanks`). Weeks come from `assignWeeks`: the games are packed into 17 weeks in the order they are listed, which always fits, and then the weeks are shuffled so the six division games are spread through the season, never two weeks in a row (all teams play their division games in the same weeks). Custom leagues use a plain round-robin instead. Covered by `test_sched.js`.

## Your players' stats
The Stats tab (campaign and leagues) opens with a **Your players** panel: passing, rushing, receiving and defensive lines for your team for the season so far, any single game this season, any past season, or your career with the team. A team picker on the same panel shows any other team's players too; for teams nobody runs only this season's totals exist, so single games, past seasons and career are offered only for teams a person runs. Totals for the whole league are kept as before, but game-by-game lines are only stored for teams a person runs (so saves stay small), and each season's line is archived on the player when the season ends. Games played before this feature existed have totals but no game lines.

## Draft picks in trades
Every team always owns one pick per round for the next **three** drafts it can still trade (7 rounds in the campaign, the league's rookie-round setting in leagues): during the season that is this year's draft and the next two; once this year's draft starts, its picks lock and a fourth year appears right away, so three years stay tradeable through the draft, preseason and the next season. Picks appear above the player lists on the Trades tab, one row per year, one chip per round; the number on each chip is its trade value and hovering shows the expected overall slot. A pick can be part of any trade, with CPU teams or between managers, alone or mixed with players (up to 8 assets each way). Each pick's value (`pickValue` in `lib/league.js`, same units as `playerValue`) comes from three things: the **round** (value follows the rookie a pick in that slot is expected to produce), the **projected finish** of the team it originally belongs to (for this year's draft: lineup rating, blended with the record as the season goes on, and the real draft order once the season is over; for later drafts: the team's **overall rating** alone, so the lowest-rated team's future picks project first in each round and the highest-rated team's last), and nothing else: picks for later drafts are not discounted for being further away, so a team's pick keeps its value from year to year. When the rookie draft runs, each slot goes to whoever owns that pick and spent picks disappear. Once the draft has started, that year's picks cannot be traded. Covered by `test_picks.js`, `ui_picks.py` and the pick checks in `test_api.js`.

Note: a live snap costs roughly 10 Redis commands per second per controlling player while it runs (about 100-200 commands for a snap), so a game played entirely live uses a few thousand.

## Deploy
1. Push this folder to GitHub, import it in Vercel. Framework: **Other**, no build command, output directory `public` (already in `vercel.json`; `public/index.html` is committed pre-built).
2. Add **Upstash Redis** from the Vercel Marketplace (it sets `KV_REST_API_URL` / `KV_REST_API_TOKEN`, or `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL` / `_TOKEN`). Without it the functions use per-instance memory, which only works for local testing.
3. Redeploy.

**Cloudflare instead of Upstash:** create a D1 database (Cloudflare dashboard → Storage & Databases → D1), then add three Vercel environment variables: `CF_ACCOUNT_ID`, `CF_D1_DATABASE_ID`, and `CF_API_TOKEN` (an API token with *D1: Edit*). The table is created automatically. If these are set, they're used instead of Upstash. Note Cloudflare's REST API allows about 1,200 requests per 5 minutes per token, which caps how many people can have a league or tournament page open at once (each polls every 5 s); Upstash has no such cap.

Accounts are name + password (scrypt) with a 30-day session token. Nothing else is stored about people.

## How results stay honest
Games are deterministic: every client and the server replay `seed + squads + picks`. CPU opponents are deterministic too, so a human-vs-CPU result is verified server-side from the human's picks alone; human-vs-human results are replayed from the picks stored in the room. A reported result that doesn't replay to a finished game is rejected. Quick-game and tournament drafts are validated on the server (turn order, one owner per player, slot limits, cap).

## Costs / limits
Leagues and tournaments poll a tiny version key every 5 s while open. Free-tier Upstash (10k commands/day) is enough for casual groups; raise `ms` in `poll()` (`src/hub/01_core.js`) if you hit it. League documents are stored gzipped (~100-300 KB).

```
node test_league.js && node test_custom.js && node test_api.js && node test_live.js && node test_clock.js && node test_plays.js && node test_ret.js && node test_roster.js && node test_offers.js && node test_block.js
python3 mkfast.py && node dev-server.js 5055 public_fast   # then: python3 ui_live.py (human vs human, live), ui_live_cpu.py, ui_ret.py (punt and kickoff returns by hand), ui_kick.py / ui_kick2.py (the kickoff choice vs the CPU and online)
```
- `lib/live.js` the on-field simulation (`src/live_client.js` is its renderer, input and lockstep layer); `lib/engine.js` shared game engine; `lib/league.js` season/draft/trade/bracket logic (browser + server); `lib/draft.js` snake-draft board, salary model and CPU drafter; `lib/data.json` real players (built by `build_data.py` from nflverse depth charts, rosters and schedule + EA Madden ratings; players without a published rating inherit comparable players' ratings).
- Skin/hair per player are approximate; sprites only vary in those two (plus team colors).
- Unofficial fan project, no logos.
